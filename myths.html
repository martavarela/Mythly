Trojan War Part 1

So it all started with this massive wedding between Thetis (a sea-nymph) and Peleus (some rando mortal.). Literally everyone on Olympus got an invite. Except Eris. Eris is the goddess of chaos and messing stuff up, so obviously nobody wanted her ruining the vibe.

But if you think Eris is just going to sit at home and pout, you don't know Eris. She decided to crash the party anyway, and she brought the worst wedding gift ever: a glowing golden apple.

Right as the reception was going off, she popped up in the middle of the dancefloor. "Listen up!" she yelled, holding out the shiny fruit. "This apple goes to the absolute most beautiful goddess in the room." Then she just yeeted the apple into the crowd and sprinted out the door.

Absolute chaos. Goddesses were throwing elbows trying to catch it, but it miraculously bounced right into Zeus' lap. He looked at the little tag on it. "For the fairest," it read.

"Pass it here, Dad," Athena said. "She was totally making eye contact when she threw it."

"Excuse you," Hera interrupted. "Zeus, dude, I'm the literal queen of the gods. Hand it to your loving wife before things get ugly."

"Um, literally what are you guys talking about?" Aphrodite chimed in. "I'm the actual goddess of beauty. It's my whole thing."

Zeus was sweating buckets. If he picked one, the other two would make his immortal life a living nightmare forever. There was zero way to win, so he did what any great leader would do: he shoved the problem onto someone else.

"I can't possibly judge this! I'm way too biased," Zeus lied smoothly. "You guys need someone totally neutral."

The three goddesses glared at him, but agreed. "Fine," Hera crossed her arms. "Who exactly is this 'fair' judge going to be?"

Zeus panicked, looking frantically out the window. Down on Earth, he spotted a totally random, ordinary shepherd just chilling with his sheep. "Him!" Zeus yelled, pointing at the poor guy. "That guy right there!"

Thrilled to not be the bad guy, Zeus told Hermes to go handle it. Boom—Hermes teleported right in front of the clueless shepherd, totally ruining his peaceful afternoon.

Trojan War Part 2

"So", Hermes began, "I'm totes sorry to ruin your wonderful time but some stuff has to happen when you're Zeus' messenger. Anyway, Eris has decided to crash another wedding and there have been some chain reactions, some is an understatement but the point is, Aphrodite, Athena and Hera are all fighting over who is the most beautiful and you're going to judge them. Awesome right." Hermes said desperately trying to keep it cool. So this shepherd guy was not just a shepherd guy, he was the ultimate prince of Troy, Paris. But Paris didn't know, he thought he was just a normal shepherd. At this point, you're probably thinking, "Hey I want to know more about this random Paris dude."

So Paris was the son of King Priam and Queen Hecuba of Troy, they were friends with literally everyone. Like they were the kind of people who everyone is friends with. When they had Paris, they heard a prophecy saying that Paris would burn the whole city of Troy. So, guess that was pretty annoying. The King and Queen were loyal to their city, so, even though they loved Paris, he had to be sacrificed. However, King Priam just wouldn't be able to be believe it if he just killed Paris on the spot. His servant had to do the job. He ordered his servant, Agileus, to abandon the baby in the mountains. This servant, like pretty much everyone else, loved Priam and eventually agreed to do it. However, like Priam, Agileus couldn't kill him. He instead, raised him and taught him to be a shepherd.

Paris had a great life and eventually married a nymph called Oynone.

So back to the beauty contest. Paris was pretty shocked, it isn't every day you judge three major goddesses. However, rejecting a task from the gods is never smart, so he agreed. Out of nowhere, three divine beings appeared in front of him, they literally radiated power. They were all pretty desperate to win the apple or they were just cheaters because they started speaking into his mind.

"I'm the Queen of the Gods, without me society falls, Olympus descends into chaos. In some ways, I am the most important goddess, pick me and you can become the king of literally everywhere," Hera whispered.

That sounded pretty good but there were still others.

"I am the goddess of wisdom, you would be known for your intelligence and knowledge, have you ever wanted to be as cunning as Oddyseus or as resourceful as Perseus?" Athena spoke.

"My son is Eros, I command over him, his arrows can make anyone fall in love. You could marry this woman," Aphrodite said.

A woman with incredible beauty was cast into his mind.

"That is Helen of Sparta," Aphrodite said, "You will surprisingly find her in Sparta. If you pick me and you find her, I promise she will love you."

Paris was clearly not too faithful to Oynoni as he chose Aphrodite and set off for Sparta. There probably was chaos among the gods but we're going to wrap it up there.

The Fate of Oedipus Part 1

This story starts with a prophecy. This prophecy stated that if King Laius of Thebes were to father a son, the son would grow up to kill him and marry his queen, Jocasta of Thebes. So King Laius of Thebes was pretty bummed.

Eventually, the temptation to have a child became too much for Laius and he had one. Immediately after, Laius told his shepherd to leave Oedipus on mount Cithaeron to die. King Laius also pinned the boy's ankles together. I mean, to me that sounds pretty useless, having a baby so it gets killed afterwards.

But the shepherd couldn't bring himself to kill the child. So the shepherd of Thebes gave him to a shepherd in Corinth. Then this shepherd passed him on to the king and queen of Corinth. Since all the events in the past had happened well before his memory, he believed his mother and father were the king and queen of Corinth. They name him Oedipus meaning swollen ankle because of how Laius pinned his ankle.

One day, Oedipus was at the palace of the king and queen of Corinth when a drunk man approached him. The drunk mant told him that he was not the son of the king and queen of Corinth. Oedipus decided to visit the oracle at Delphi to find out more. The oracle told Oedipus the prophecy but did not answer the question as to whether he was the son of the king and queen of Corinth. Oedipus was shocked. He didn't want to marry and kill the king and queen of Corinth who he believed were the ones in the prophecy. Eventually, Oedipus decided to go somewhere else to lessen the chances of killing and marrying the king and queen.

Unfortunately, the place Oedipus chose to go to was Thebes. On the path to Thebes, he met the king. The king demanded that Oedipus make way for him. Oedipus was like, "No way, dude, I was here first." They quarreled for a while, and eventually Oedipus killed the king, unaware that this was his father.

Next, Oedipus fell in love with the king's wife, Jocasta. Anyone who wanted to marry Jocasta had to solve a riddle set by the sphinx. The sphinx was terrorising the city of Thebes and wouldn't go until someone correctly solved its riddle. The city was so despearate that anyone who solved the riddle could marry the queen of Thebes. The sphinx had the head of a person, the body of a lion, and the wings of an eagle.

Sphinxes were known for their fiendishly tricky riddles and puzzles. Anyway, the sphinx asked Oedipus, "What has four legs in the morning, two legs in the afternoon, and three legs in the evening?" Oedipus didn't hesitate. "A person," he said, "a person crawls when they are a baby, walks on two feet when they are grown up, and uses a walking stick when they are elderly." The sphinx was so embarssed that it jumped off a cliff and Oedipus and Jocasta got married.

Oedipus and Jocasta got married.

The Fate of Oedipus Part 2

Oedipus was a hero in Thebes for killing the Sphinx. He and Jocasta had two sons named Polyneices and Eteocles and two daughters named Antigone and Ismene.

One day a plague hits Thebes, and the citizens are desperate for a solution. Oedipus takes it upon himself to find the cause of the plague and save his people. He sends Jocasta's brother, Creon, to investigate. Now Creon was the man who had suggested that Jocasta should marry whoever solved the riddle so he was known for his wisdom.

Creon tells Oedipus that the plague is a punishment for the murder of Laius, and that the murderer must be found and punished. Unaware that he is the murderer, Oedipus curses the murderer.

There was a blind prophet named Tiresias who could see the future. He was called upon to help solve the mystery of the plague. He originally refused to speak, afraid of angering Oedipus but when Oedipus accuses Tiresias of being the murderer, he tells Oedipus that Oedipus himself is the murderer.

Oedipus accused Tiresias of conspiring with Creon to steal the throne.

Jocasta comforts Oedipus by telling him that prophecies are fake and that he should not be so afraid.

She tells Oedipus that the son in the prophecy was killed ages ago and that Laius was killed by a group of bandits where three roads meet.

While Oedipus had killed almost all of Laius' servants, he had spared one. He called for him.

While waiting for the servant a messenger came saying that the king of Corinth was dead of old age. Since, Oedipus was still in belief that the king of Corinth was the one in the prophecy, he was glad he had not killed him.

The messenger then told Oedipus that the king and queen of Corinth were not his real parents so he had nothing to fear. Now this messenger knew this because he was the Corinthian shepherd who had been given Oedipus by the Thebian Shepherd.

Oedipus was relieved to hear this, but he was still curious about his true parentage. He asked the messenger to tell him more about his past.

Soon the servant Oedipus had summoned arrived and the servant was the Thebian shepherd who had given Oedipus to the Corinthian shepherd.

Oedipus remembered that Jocasta had told him that Laius was killed by a group of bandits where three roads meet. He had killed someone where three roads meet. The Thebian shepherd confirmed Oedipus' past and the truth was revealed.

Jocasta hung herself in despair. When Oedipus found her, he uses her brooches to blind himself.
